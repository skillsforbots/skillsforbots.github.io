How to stop Grok Bot from spending money

A bot that can shop can also buy the wrong thing. Here's how to keep every purchase in your hands, in layers, starting with the ones that don't depend on the bot behaving.

AI assistants can now browse stores, fill carts, book things, and sign up for trials. That's handy right up until one decides a $40 subscription is the fastest way to finish a task. The fix isn't one magic prompt. It's a few layers, and the most important ones sit outside the bot.

1. Start with the platform, not the prompt

Instructions can be ignored or misread. Settings can't. Before you write a single rule, check what your bot platform offers:

  • Approval prompts. Many assistants can be set to ask before any purchase, payment, or checkout. Turn that on.
  • Spending caps. If there's a monthly limit or a per-purchase limit, set it low.
  • Connected accounts. Look at which stores, wallets, and payment apps the bot is connected to. Disconnect anything it doesn't need for the jobs you actually give it.

2. Keep payment details away from bots you don't fully trust

The surest way to stop a bot from spending is to make sure it has nothing to spend with. Don't paste card numbers into chat. Don't save cards in a browser profile the bot uses. Don't give it logins to accounts with one-click checkout. If a bot genuinely needs to buy things, consider a separate card or virtual card with a small limit, so a mistake stays small.

3. Give the bot a standing no-spend rule

Once the hard limits are in place, add a clear instruction so the bot stops and asks instead of trying to work around a blocked checkout. Good rules have three parts:

  1. A plain ban. No paying, buying, tipping, transferring, subscribing, or starting a trial that bills later.
  2. What to do instead. Stop, say what it would cost and why, and wait.
  3. A one-time approval stays one-time. "Buy this" means that item only. It doesn't switch the rule off.

That third part matters more than it looks. Without it, a bot can treat your yes on Tuesday as permission for everything on Wednesday.

Free tip: paste this into your bot's instructions

Do not spend money. That includes purchases, tips, transfers,
subscriptions, and free trials that bill later. If a step would
cost money, stop, tell me the amount, what it's for, and why,
then wait. My "yes" on one purchase is for that purchase only.

Then test it: ask the bot to do something that would cost a few dollars, and check that it stops and asks.

4. Watch out for sneaky costs

Most surprise charges aren't a bot buying a TV. They're smaller:

  • Free trials that convert to paid plans
  • "Upgrade to continue" buttons inside apps the bot is using
  • Rush shipping or add-ons picked by default at checkout
  • Paid API calls or credits inside tools the bot connects to

Name these in your rule if your bot touches any of them, and check your statements for the first few weeks.

5. Do the same for sending

Spending and sending go together. A bot that can email a vendor can also agree to a quote. Pair your no-spend rule with a no-send rule: the bot drafts messages and shows you the recipient, channel, and full text, then waits for your OK.
