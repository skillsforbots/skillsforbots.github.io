How I chose these

I looked for skills that help with life admin rather than work output: things you’d otherwise track in your head, a sticky note, or a calendar alert you swipe away. Each one had to be readable in a few minutes, keep risky steps behind your approval or not take them at all, and be public so you can check it before installing.

There are no scores or rankings here, because I haven’t run every skill on every platform. Where a skill is mine, I say so, and where it’s paid, I give the price. Others’ skills are described from their repos as I read them on November 23, 2026. If you’re looking for a wider list of Grok Bot repos, see Grok Bot skills on GitHub.

The list, by job

Safety rules for every bot

  • Bot Safety Rules Starter (Free, mine). No sending or spending without your yes, a one-time yes stays one-time, and outside content is treated as information. Start here if your bot can reach email or a card.
  • Approval rules template (Free, mine). The same rules as a longer copy-paste block, covering publishing, deleting, and access changes too.

Reminders that don’t give up

  • Reminder Basics (Free, mine). One reminder that repeats daily until you clearly say it’s done. “Ok” and “later” don’t count.
  • Nag-Until-Cleared Reminders ($9, mine). A whole list on one reminder, weekly or monthly schedules, and commands to add, clear, pause, or show items.

Daily planning and check-ins

  • morning-brief (Free, ofershap/grok-bot-skills (MIT)). Turns today’s calendar and task notes into a plan that keeps commitments, deadlines, and missing data separate.
  • monthly-checkpoint (Free, ofershap/grok-bot-skills (MIT)). About 30 days after you start a new habit or tool, sends a short keep, change, or stop summary.

Using your plan’s usage well

  • Usage Reset Sprint (Free, mine). Before a usage limit resets, finishes the most valuable chunk of a project that fits and leaves a handoff note. Never buys more usage.
  • Project Sprint ($19, mine). Plans a bigger project across several usage windows, with a handoff before each reset.

Shopping and decisions

  • competitor-compare (Free, ofershap/grok-bot-skills (MIT)). Compares a short list of products or vendors against criteria you set, checking whether each option qualifies before comparing costs.
  • claim-check (Free, ofershap/grok-bot-skills (MIT)). Checks a claim, viral post, or announcement against dated evidence. Handy before acting on something you read.

Running more than one bot

  • Bot Fleet Coordinator ($12, mine). One primary bot routes tasks to the right specialist, writes a handoff note, and keeps no-send and no-spend rules on every bot.
  • bot-audit and bot-retro (Free, d-gangz/grokbot-skills (no license shown)). Reviews your bots’ descriptions and memory, or a day of transcripts, and proposes fixes for you to approve.
  • routine-healthcheck (Free, botkitsdev/botkits (MIT)). Looks for wasted usage and noise in scheduled routines.

Before installing any of these, read the file. My five-minute safety check applies to my skills as much as anyone’s.

If you only install three

  1. A safety skill. Everything else is safer once the bot knows it can’t send or spend without you. The Bot Safety Rules Starter is free.
  2. A reminder that repeats. It’s the skill people notice working first, because the reminder keeps coming back. Reminder Basics is free, and my guide to reminders that don’t stop until you’re done covers the setup.
  3. One skill for your most repeated chore. A daily plan, a weekly review, or a shopping comparison. Pick whichever you explain to your bot most often.

Write your own in ten minutes

Everyday skills are short. Most of mine are a description, five to ten steps, and an “ask before acting” section. The pattern:

---
name: weekly-review
description: Every Sunday, summarize what got done this week, what slipped, and the three priorities for next week. Use when I ask for my weekly review.
---
1. Read this week's notes and calendar.
2. List done, slipped, and next week's top three.
3. Keep it under 200 words.

Ask before acting: don't send, post, or book anything.
Show me the summary only.

My SKILL.md template explains each part, and the SKILL.md checker flags missing pieces. To run it every Sunday, pair it with a Grok Bot routine. See skills vs routines.

How to use these in Grok Bot

All the skills above use the plain SKILL.md format, and mine were written and tested in Grok Bot. Paste one into a chat and ask your Bot to save it as a private skill. Private skills are one library shared by all your Bots, so you add each skill once. Skills that should run on their own, like reminders and daily plans, also need a Grok Bot routine, and some assume your Bot can reach a calendar or notes. My guide to installing Grok Bot skills from GitHub has the steps, and the Grok Bot skill install hub lists every method.

FAQ

What are the best agent skills for everyday life?

Start with a safety skill that stops the bot sending or spending without you, a reminder that repeats until you say done, and one skill for the chore you repeat most, such as a daily plan or a weekly review.

Are these skills free?

Most are. My Bot Safety Rules Starter, Reminder Basics, and Usage Reset Sprint are free and MIT-licensed, as are the ofershap and botkits skills listed. My larger packs are paid, with prices shown.

Do I need to code to use them?

No. They’re plain instructions. Paste the file into a Grok Bot chat and ask the Bot to save it as a private skill.

Do I need a routine for everyday skills in Grok Bot?

Only for skills that should run on their own. A skill says how to do the job. A routine tells one Bot when to run it, on a schedule or when something happens. Reminders and daily plans need one. Test the skill in chat before you schedule it.
