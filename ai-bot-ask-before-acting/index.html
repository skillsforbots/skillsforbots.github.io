How to make your AI bot ask before it acts

Helpful bots try to finish the job. Here's how to make yours draft the email, show the cost, or show the post, then wait for a clear yes.

Bots are trained to be useful. If you say "tell them I'll be late" or "go ahead and book it," a lot of assistants will send the message or finish the checkout because that looks like completing the task. A standing rule fixes that: the bot prepares the action, shows you everything you need to decide, and waits.

1. Name the actions that need a yes

Start with anything you can't easily undo:

  • Sending email, chat messages, texts, or posts
  • Paying, buying, tipping, subscribing, or starting a trial that bills later
  • Deleting files, changing account settings, or publishing anything under your name

Write that list into the bot's standing instructions. If you only ban spending, it can still send a messy email. If you only ban sending, it can still click buy. Cover both. There's a separate guide on stopping a bot from spending money; the steps below work for sending and for any other action you want held for approval.

2. Write a rule with three parts

Good approval rules are short and specific:

  1. A plain ban. No sending, posting, or spending unless you clearly approve that item.
  2. What to do instead. Stop. For a send, show the recipient, channel, and full text. For a payment, show the amount, what it's for, and why. Then wait.
  3. A one-time yes stays one-time. "Send this" or "buy this" means that item only. It does not switch the rule off.

Add one more line: a draft is not permission. Without that, a bot can treat "looks good" or the act of drafting as a green light.

Free tip: paste this into your bot's instructions

Do not send email, chat messages, texts, or posts, and do not
spend money, without my explicit OK. If a step would send or
cost money, stop, show me the recipient, channel, and full text
(or the amount, what it's for, and why), then wait. My "yes"
on one item is for that item only. A draft is not permission.

Then test it: ask the bot to email someone, or to buy something small, and check that it stops and asks.

3. Test it on purpose

A rule you have never tested is only a hope. Ask the bot to do something that would send a message or cost a few dollars. Confirm it stops, shows the details, and waits. Then approve that one item and make a second request. The standing rule should still be on. If a single "yes" turns it off, tighten the third part and test again.

4. Turn on platform approvals too

Instructions can be ignored or misread. Settings can't. If your bot platform can require an approval prompt before sending a message, posting, or paying, turn that on. The written rule is the backup, not the only layer.
