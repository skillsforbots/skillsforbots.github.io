How to get a daily credit action plan from an AI bot

A bot can give you a short, repeatable plan for what to do today on a credit file. Here's how to set that up so it coaches, estimates, and drafts, but never pretends it can act in your name.

An AI bot can be a useful credit coach if you give it a fixed brief format and some hard limits. It can remind you what to do today, label every figure as an estimate, and draft letters for you to review. It should not invent report details, promise a score gain, send a letter, or pay anything on your behalf.

What a useful brief looks like

Keep each run short and repeatable, so you can check it quickly:

  1. A clear disclaimer at the top, in plain language, like the one above.
  2. Today's top 3 actions, ranked by what is likely to matter first for the goal you named, such as an auto loan or a later mortgage.
  3. For each action: why it is on the list, which score model it is aimed at if you know it, and an impact or speed range clearly labeled as an estimate.
  4. Drafts you can review and send yourself, when a letter would help. The bot stops at the draft.

If the bot does not have your reports, it should say so and give general guidance marked as general, not a plan for your file.

What to share, and what to keep back

Share only facts you actually have: the goal, any scores and which model they came from, statement dates, due dates, and items you believe are wrong or not yours. If something is unknown, leave it unknown. Don't let the bot fill gaps with guesses.

Don't paste card numbers, bank logins, or one-click payment access into the chat. The bot can tell you to pull your reports at AnnualCreditReport.com and to make a payment yourself. It does not need to log in for you.

Rules the bot has to follow

Put these in its standing instructions, not just in one chat:

  • Never invent scores, balances, dates, or report items.
  • Never promise a score gain, an approval, a deletion, or a timeline. If a number appears, label it an estimate.
  • Draft only. You pull reports, you send letters, you make payments.
  • Dispute only what is inaccurate or not yours. Disputing accurate negatives as a tactic should be out of bounds.

Setting it up

  1. Pick one bot to act as the coach, so you aren't comparing conflicting plans.
  2. Give it standing instructions with your goal, the brief format, and the rules above.
  3. Run it daily or on demand. A scheduled routine works if you want a regular check-in. The same prompt also works when you paste it yourself.
  4. Tell it what changed after you take an action, so the next brief is not a repeat of the last one.

Read every draft against your own reports before you send or pay. If a figure looks off, ask where it came from, or ignore it.

Free tip: a first prompt you can paste

Give me a short credit action brief. Start with: this is
education, not financial advice, not legal advice, and not
credit repair company services; no score gain or approval
is promised; any figures are estimates only.

Use only the facts I provide. If something is missing, write
Unknown and do not invent it. Give today's top 3 actions,
each with why and an estimated impact or speed range labeled
as an estimate. Draft any letter I might send, but do not
send letters, file disputes, or pay anything. Then stop.

Follow that with the goal and the facts you actually have. If the bot skips the disclaimer, invents a number, or offers to send the letter, tighten the instructions and test again.
